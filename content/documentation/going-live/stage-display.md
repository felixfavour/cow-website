*Free feature.* A dedicated screen for the people on stage (musicians, vocalists, speakers) showing what's live, what's coming next, a countdown timer, and the time of day, without any of the Control Center's editing tools visible.

## Opening it

Open it with the **Open Stage Display** quick action, or by assigning a monitor to it in **Settings → Display Settings**. See [Multi-screen setup](/docs/going-live/multi-screen-setup).

## Four panels

| Panel | Shows |
|-------|-------|
| **Now** | The text of whatever slide is currently live |
| **Next** | The text of the upcoming slide in the schedule |
| **Timer** | A count for the current slide, with start, pause and reset controls on the stage screen itself. When a countdown slide is live it follows that countdown and turns **red** as it runs out; otherwise it shows where you are in the schedule, for example *Slide 4 of 21* |
| **Clock** | The current time |

The text in **Now** and **Next** resizes itself to fill the space available, so a short line is large and a long verse still fits without scrolling. On a narrow or portrait screen the panels stack vertically instead of the normal 2-column grid, so a TV mounted in portrait works well.

Like the Live Display, double-click anywhere on the Stage Display to toggle full screen (or press **F**). This also removes the banner reminding you it's the stage display.

::callout{type="tip" title="Safe to hand off"}
Nothing on the Stage Display can edit a slide or take one live. The only controls on it are the timer's start, pause and reset.
::

## Alerts and overlays do not appear here

[Alerts and overlays](/docs/going-live/alerts-and-overlays) reach the congregation's screen and the livestream, and are deliberately kept off the Stage Display. The band does not need the car park announcement covering their lyrics.

## It runs from the operator's computer

The Stage Display is fed directly by the Control Center on the same machine, so plug the stage screen into the presentation computer rather than signing in on a separate device. You can run more than one from that computer, for example one for the worship team and one for the preacher.

Controlling the service from a separate device is a different job. See [using CoW on your phone](/docs/teams-and-collaboration/using-cloud-of-worship-on-your-phone).
