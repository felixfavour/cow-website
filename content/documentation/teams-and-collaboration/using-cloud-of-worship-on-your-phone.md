*Teams feature.*

Open `app.cloudofworship.com` on a phone and you get a full operator, laid out for a small screen. Not a viewer, not a remote with three buttons: the same Control Center, the same Quick Actions, the same schedule.

This is for the moments that do not happen at the media desk. The pastor changes the closing song. A scripture is needed that was not in the plan. The person who knows the software is currently leading worship.

## The layout

The phone opens on the **slide grid**, which is your schedule. Tapping a slide selects it, exactly as on desktop. Three buttons along the bottom bring up the rest, one at a time, and tapping the button you are already on returns you to the grid.

| Tab | What it is |
|-----|------------|
| **Quick Actions** | The desktop left column, given the whole screen: Bible, songs, hymns, media, My Library, templates, countdowns, PDF import |
| **Live** | The live preview, the slide schedule under it, and the live output menu |
| **Schedules** | Switching which service you are working on |

Anything you create on the phone reaches the rest of your team instantly, and their slides reach you. A phone is a full member of the schedule. See [working as a team](/docs/teams-and-collaboration/working-as-a-team).

## Driving the projector from a phone

Creating slides is one thing. Changing what the congregation is looking at is another, and Cloud of Worship is deliberately careful here.

A phone does not broadcast to every screen in the building. It picks **one specific output** and drives that one. In a room where several laptops have the schedule open, a change made from a phone lands on the projector and nowhere else.

### On the computer driving the projector

1. **Open the live window.** A device only offers itself for control while its live window is actually open. A laptop merely viewing the schedule is not an output.
2. Open **Settings → Display Settings**.
3. Check that **Let a team member's phone control this output** is on. It is on by default, and it is stored on that computer only, so switching it off at the media desk changes nothing for anyone else.

### On the phone

At the top of the **Live** tab there is a status row telling you exactly where your slides are going:

| What it says | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Not controlling a screen** | Slides you take live only reach the livestream. Tap the row to pick an output |
| **Controlling [name]'s screen** | You are driving that device's live output. The device name is shown underneath |
| **Reconnecting to the output…** | That screen dropped off. Slides are paused until it comes back |

Tap the row, choose a screen from the list, and tap slides to take them live. If no computer in your team has a live window open, the list says **No output device online**.

The phone's preview mirrors what that projector is actually showing, not what the phone last asked for, so you can see whether your change landed.

::callout{type="important" title="Nobody is taken over quietly"}
Back at the media desk, the operator sees who is currently controlling the output above the slide schedule, and that indicator clears once the phone has been idle for a couple of minutes. Control itself ends when the live window on the host computer closes, or when the operator switches the setting off.
::

## The computer stays in charge

The device with the projector attached remains responsible for its own screen, and it is the device that keeps feeding [livestream viewers](/docs/going-live/livestream-output-obs-vmix). A phone borrows control of the output. It never becomes the output.

## A pattern that works

- The **media desk computer** owns the projector, the [stage display](/docs/going-live/stage-display) and the livestream feed, and stays on for the whole service.
- A **worship leader or pastor** keeps the schedule open on a phone, adding a scripture or an extra song as the service moves.
- During the sermon, whoever is following along takes passages live while the operator watches the room.

Agree who is taking slides live before the service, not during it.
