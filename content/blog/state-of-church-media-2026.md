It's Sunday, 9:47am. The slide doesn't exist yet.

We used to assume church media was planned on Saturday and simply played back on Sunday. Then we looked at two years of our own product data, every slide created, every verse put up, every Sunday morning scramble, across churches using Cloud of Worship, and the data told a different story.

This isn't a survey. It's what actually happened, measured from October 2024 to October 2026. Here's what two years of real church services taught us.

## Church media happens live, not in advance

The busiest hours for making slides are the same hours churches are already presenting live. For most churches, that's Sunday morning. For Sabbath-keeping churches, about 1 in 7 of the regular churches we looked at, it's Saturday, and the pattern holds just as well: the slides arrive with the congregation, not before it.

A few numbers from the day of worship, in GMT+1:

- **3 in 4** Sunday slides are made between 7am and 1pm, while the service is already running.
- **68%** of all live presenting happens on a Sunday.
- Sunday makes up **51%** of a whole week's slides. Saturday, mostly Sabbath services plus next-day prep, is **16%**. Wednesday and Friday midweek services sit around 8 to 9%. Monday is the quietest day at just **4%**. The media team has earned it.

## What's actually on the screen

Most church presentation software was built around song lyrics. Our data says something different: scripture takes the biggest share of the screen.

Across every slide made in two years: **34% Bible verses**, 30% songs and hymns, 19% text and notices, 16% photos and video, 2% countdowns. During live services, scripture's share climbs even higher, to roughly half of everything shown.

### The verses churches put up most

Ranked by how many churches projected each one (starter templates and default verses left out):

1. John 3:16
2. Isaiah 43:19, "Behold, I will do a new thing"
3. Joshua 1:8
4. Jeremiah 29:11
5. Romans 12:1–2
6. Isaiah 40:31
7. John 10:10
8. Proverbs 3:5
9. Malachi 3:10, offering time, you know the one
10. Psalm 91:1

**KJV is still the default Bible in 9 out of 10 churches.** Beyond that, churches project the Bible in 29 different translations, including Yoruba, Swahili, Twi, Hausa, Pidgin, French, Spanish and Indonesian.

### The songs and hymns sharing a setlist

Among the songs the most churches put on screen: **Holy Forever**, Worthy of My Praise, Goodness of God, Still, Way Maker, Yahweh Sabaoth, Eze Ebube, Great Are You Lord, This Kind God, and What a Beautiful Name. Global worship and African worship sit on the same Sunday setlist without anyone holding a meeting about it.

And the hymns refuse to retire. **Blessed Assurance**, written by Fanny Crosby in 1873, is on more church screens than any song released this year. Great Is Thy Faithfulness, To God Be the Glory, Count Your Blessings, There Shall Be Showers of Blessing, Amazing Grace and What a Friend We Have in Jesus round out the list.

## One laptop, a long Sunday, and a lot of "Untitled" schedules

A typical service puts about **40 slides** on the screen. The longest 1 in 10 services pass **180 slides**, which is usually what happens when "one last song" turns into six.

We also found that **620 churches, nearly 1 in 5**, have run a full service from a schedule still called "Untitled Schedule" or "Untitled Regular Sunday." It fits the rest of the data. Naming the service can wait. The service can't.

On the hardware side: **8 in 10 operators run on Windows**, mostly Chrome or Edge, and the most common screen width is under 1300 pixels. Church media mostly runs on the laptop someone donated a few years back, and it works.

The phone is quickly becoming the second screen. The number of operators controlling slides from their phone grew **4.7 times** between June and October 2026. That's the media desk moving into a pocket, so someone can run the service from the aisle, the choir stand, or the seat next to the pastor.

## Church media is going global

Churches in **63 countries** have presented live with Cloud of Worship. Nigeria leads, followed by the United States, Ghana, the United Kingdom, Kenya, Uganda, South Africa and the Philippines, the same songs and verses, very different time zones.

Asia is warming up fast. Live slides shown by churches across 12 Asian countries, including the Philippines, India, Indonesia and the UAE, nearly tripled this year, and October alone was busier than the whole of January through March.

Overall, churches using Cloud of Worship made about **8 times** as many slides this summer as they did the summer before.

## Where church media is headed

A few forecasts, each backed by the data above:

**Preparation moves into the service.** Slides get made while the congregation is already singing, so tools will be judged by how fast they react, not by how many fonts they have.

**The software starts listening.** 182 churches have already let Cloud of Worship listen in and surface scriptures as the sermon mentions them, over 6,000 minutes of preaching and counting. The volunteer stops chasing the preacher and starts keeping pace.

**Screens keep putting scripture first.** With scripture at roughly half of everything shown live, church software built mainly around lyrics will have to catch up to what's actually on the screen.

**Every language gets a seat.** Many of our failed song searches are in Swahili, Twi and Igbo. Bilingual slides, a song in the local language with the English underneath, are already becoming normal.

**The hymnbook stays.** KJV, an 1873 hymn and this year's biggest release can all show up in the same service. The tools that win will hold all three in one search box.

**The projector and the livestream become one screen.** 77 churches streamed their service in the first month we offered livestreaming. The room and the stream are merging into a single output.

**Bigger teams, lighter Sundays.** The growth in phone-based remote control says it plainly: church media is moving toward a small rota sharing the load, not one person carrying it alone. And if it's just you running things for now, Cloud of Worship is built to carry you too.
