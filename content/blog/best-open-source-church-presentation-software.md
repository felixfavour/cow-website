Most churches don't go looking for **open source church presentation software** because they care about licenses. They go looking because the budget is tight, the media laptop is old, and they need lyrics and Bible verses on the screen by Sunday.

Open source tools answer that need well. They cost nothing, they don't lock your songs inside a company's format, and when something breaks there is usually a community of other church volunteers who have hit the same problem.

This guide walks through the best open source options for churches in 2026. We build one of them, Cloud of Worship, so we'll be upfront about where it fits and where another tool may serve you better. Every project on this list is doing good work for the Church, and the right choice depends on your team, your hardware and your services.

## Quick answer :span{.as-h3}

- **Best for teams that prepare together and want to work from any computer:** Cloud of Worship
- **Best long-standing, fully free desktop tool:** OpenLP
- **Best modern desktop app with a lot of creative control:** FreeShow
- **Best lightweight option for older computers, if you're happy with Java:** Quelea
- **Best for churches with a lot of history in its format:** OpenSong

## What to look for in open source worship software :span{.as-h3}

Before comparing tools, it helps to know what actually matters on a Sunday morning.

1. **Songs and lyrics.** How easy is it to add a song, split it into verses and choruses, and reorder it live when the worship leader repeats the bridge?
2. **Bible display.** Which translations are included, can you show two versions side by side, and how fast can you find a verse while the preacher is already reading it?
3. **Live output.** Does it handle a projector, a second screen, a stage display for the musicians, and a feed for your livestream?
4. **Hardware and platform.** Will it run on the computer you already own, whether that's Windows, macOS, Linux or a Chromebook?
5. **Team workflow.** Can more than one person prepare the service, or does everything have to be typed on the one booth computer?
6. **Offline use.** What happens when the church Wi-Fi drops in the middle of worship?
7. **Project health.** Is the project still being updated, and is there somewhere to get help?

Keep these questions in mind as you read the list below.

## 1. Cloud of Worship :span{.as-h3}

**Platforms:** Any modern browser (Windows, macOS, Linux, ChromeOS), plus a desktop app for Windows
**License:** GNU AGPL v3, code on [GitHub](https://github.com/CoW-Labs/cloudofworship)
**Price:** Free plan for one operator. Teams plan for collaboration

[Cloud of Worship](https://app.cloudofworship.com/) is a church presentation app that runs in your browser. There is nothing to install: sign in, build your schedule and open the live display on your projector or second screen. A Windows desktop app is there for churches that prefer one, and it adds [NDI output](/docs/going-live/ndi-output) for livestream switchers.

We started building it because many of the churches we serve, especially across Africa, run their services on modest laptops and unreliable power and internet. Heavy desktop software that needs a strong graphics card was leaving those churches behind.

**What it does well**

- **Works on the computer you have.** Because it runs in the browser, it works on Windows, Mac, Linux and Chromebooks, including older machines.
- **Offline ready.** Once your service is loaded, it keeps running if the internet drops, and you can save Bible translations for [offline use](/docs/settings-and-shortcuts/offline-use).
- **Built-in hymns and songs.** More than 3,000 hymns come built in, and you can search a shared library of thousands of worship songs from churches around the world.
- **Scripture in many languages.** Show the Bible in English and a heart language such as Yoruba, Igbo or Twi side by side. See [how multi-version scripture works](/blog/how-to-display-multiple-versions-of-scripture-from-yoruba-to-twi-in-one-click).
- **Team preparation.** Your pastor, worship leader and media team can all prepare the same service from their own devices, with changes appearing for everyone in real time.
- **Livestream ready.** Send your slides into OBS or vMix with a [livestream URL](/docs/going-live/livestream-output-obs-vmix), no capture card needed.
- **Voice and transcription.** Display Bible verses using [voice commands](/blog/display-bible-verses-faster-using-voice-commands) and sermon transcription.

**Where it may not fit**

- Some features, like team invites, stage display, slide templates and unlimited song search, are on the paid Teams plan. The free plan covers a single operator with Bible, hymns, lyrics, media and offline use. [Compare plans](/pricing).
- The desktop app is Windows only for now. Mac and Linux users run it in the browser.
- Real-time collaboration and the shared song library need an internet connection while you prepare.

**A note on the license:** Cloud of Worship is open source under the GNU AGPL v3. You can read the code, change it and run it for your church for free. If you change it and offer it to others over a network, the AGPL asks you to share your changes too, so improvements come back to every church. The Cloud of Worship name and logo aren't covered by the license. Read more about why we build in the open on our [open source page](/open-source).

**Best for:** churches with a few people preparing services, churches on older or mixed hardware, and multilingual congregations.

## 2. OpenLP :span{.as-h3}

**Platforms:** Windows, macOS, Linux
**License:** GNU GPL v2
**Price:** Free

[OpenLP](https://openlp.org/) is one of the oldest and most trusted names in free worship software. It has served churches for well over a decade, and for many volunteers it was the first church presentation software they ever used.

**What it does well**

- Completely free, with no paid tier at all.
- Songs, Bibles, images, media and presentations in one service plan.
- A web remote and stage view that musicians and preachers can open on a phone or tablet.
- Imports from many other worship programs, which makes switching easier.
- A long-standing community forum and plenty of tutorials.

**Where it may not fit**

- Preparation happens on the computer where OpenLP is installed, so teams usually share service files by hand.
- The interface feels more traditional than newer tools.
- Bible translations beyond the public domain ones usually have to be found and imported yourself.

**Best for:** single-operator churches that want a proven, fully free desktop tool. If you're weighing it up, read our [OpenLP vs Cloud of Worship comparison](/blog/openlp-vs-cloud-of-worship-which-one-is-more-practical-for-your-church).

## 3. FreeShow :span{.as-h3}

**Platforms:** Windows, macOS, Linux
**License:** GNU GPL v3
**Price:** Free

[FreeShow](https://freeshow.app/) is a modern, actively developed presentation app, now maintained under the ChurchApps nonprofit. It has quickly become one of the most popular open source worship tools on GitHub, and it is a genuinely impressive piece of work.

**What it does well**

- A modern interface with a lot of creative control over layouts, styles and transitions.
- Stage display, remote control from a phone and a mobile companion app.
- NDI output and rich media handling for churches that livestream.
- Frequent releases and an engaged community.

**Where it may not fit**

- The depth of options can feel like a lot for a brand-new volunteer.
- It is a desktop app, so preparation still happens on the machine it's installed on.
- Being built on Electron, it wants a reasonably capable computer for heavy video work.

**Best for:** churches with a confident media volunteer who wants a polished, flexible desktop tool at no cost.

## 4. Quelea :span{.as-h3}

**Platforms:** Windows, macOS, Linux
**License:** GNU GPL v3
**Price:** Free

[Quelea](https://quelea.org/) is an open source projection app focused on doing the basics well: song lyrics, Bible passages, video and PowerPoint. It has a dedicated following and is still maintained on GitHub.

**What it does well**

- Simple, focused layout that is easy to learn.
- Stage view and a mobile lyrics feature so people can follow along on their phones.
- Imports songs from EasyWorship, OpenLP, OpenSong and other formats.

**Where it may not fit**

- It is built on Java, which some churches find harder to keep updated.
- Development moves more slowly than FreeShow or Cloud of Worship.
- No built-in team workflow.

**Best for:** small churches that want a straightforward free tool and don't mind a slightly older feel.

## 5. OpenSong :span{.as-h3}

**Platforms:** Windows, macOS, Linux
**License:** GNU GPL
**Price:** Free

OpenSong was one of the pioneers of free worship software, and its song format is still supported by many of the tools above. Development has slowed a great deal in recent years, so we wouldn't recommend it for a new setup. But it deserves a place on this list for what it gave the ecosystem, and churches with years of songs in OpenSong format can move them into newer tools with little effort.

**Best for:** churches already running it that are happy with how it works.

## Free vs open source: what's the difference for a church? :span{.as-h3}

"Free" and "open source" often get used as if they mean the same thing. They don't.

- **Free software** costs nothing to download. Tools like VideoPsalm are free but closed, so you can't see or change how they work.
- **Open source software** publishes its code under a license that lets anyone use, change and share it. Cloud of Worship, OpenLP, FreeShow, Quelea and OpenSong all fall here.

For a church, the practical benefits of open source are real: you are not locked in, you can see exactly what the software does with your data, and a tech-minded volunteer can fix or improve it.

If you want to compare closed, paid tools as well, see our guides on [ProPresenter](/blog/propresenter-vs-cloud-of-worship-which-one-fits-your-church-better), [EasyWorship](/blog/cloud-of-worship-vs-easyworship-which-one-works-better-for-your-church) and [Proclaim](/blog/proclaim-vs-cloud-of-worship-which-one-is-better-for-your-church-team-copy).

## How to choose :span{.as-h3}

Ask your team three questions:

1. **Who prepares the service?** If it's one person on one computer, any tool on this list will serve you. If your pastor, worship leader and media volunteers all contribute, a tool built for teams will save hours every week.
2. **What computer do you have?** If it's an older laptop or a Chromebook, a browser-based tool is the safest bet. If it's a dedicated media PC, the desktop apps open up.
3. **Who will be running it on Sunday?** New volunteers need something they can learn in minutes. Experienced operators may enjoy the extra control of a tool like FreeShow.

The best way to decide is to try two of them on your own hardware with last Sunday's songs and scripture. You'll know within an hour which one your team prefers.

## Frequently asked questions :span{.as-h3}

### What is the best free open source church presentation software? :span{.as-h4}

For a fully free, fully open source desktop tool, OpenLP and FreeShow are the strongest choices in 2026. If you want something that runs in the browser on any computer, with team preparation and a built-in hymn and song library, Cloud of Worship is open source under the AGPL v3 and has a free plan.

### Is there an open source alternative to ProPresenter? :span{.as-h4}

Yes. FreeShow is the closest open source alternative to ProPresenter in terms of creative control and live output. Cloud of Worship covers most of what churches use ProPresenter for, including lyrics, Bible, media, stage display and livestream output, without needing a powerful Mac or PC.

### Can I use church presentation software without installing anything? :span{.as-h4}

Yes. Cloud of Worship runs entirely in a web browser, so you can sign in and present from any computer, including a Chromebook. Read more about [why browser-based software is becoming the standard](/blog/church-presentation-software-free-download-why-browser-based-is-the-new-standard-in-2026).

### Does church presentation software work without internet? :span{.as-h4}

All the tools on this list can present without internet once your service is ready. Cloud of Worship keeps your loaded service, Bible translations you've saved and your media available offline. See [offline use](/docs/settings-and-shortcuts/offline-use).

### Which software is best for showing the Bible in African languages? :span{.as-h4}

Cloud of Worship was built with multilingual churches in mind and lets you display English alongside languages like Yoruba, Igbo and Twi. See our guide to [Yoruba Bible translations for churches](/blog/yoruba-bible-translation-for-churches-creating-deeper-worship-in-nigeria).
