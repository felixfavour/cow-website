Ask an Adventist about Sabbath morning and they will often mention the singing. The hymnal is part of how the church worships, and for the media team it is part of how Sabbath runs.

This guide covers where the hymnal comes from, how it is laid out, and the easiest way to get a hymn on screen.

## A short history of Adventist hymnals

Adventists have been singing from their own books since the earliest days of the movement. In 1849, James White put together a small collection of hymns for believers who kept the commandments. Since then the church has produced several hymnals, each one for its own generation:

- **Hymns and Tunes** (1886)
- **Christ in Song** (1908)
- **The Church Hymnal** (1941)
- **The Seventh-day Adventist Hymnal** (1985)

The 1985 book is the one most congregations know today. It holds 695 hymns, and its numbers are the ones people call out on Sabbath morning.

## How the hymnal is laid out

The 1985 hymnal is organised by theme rather than by alphabet. It opens with worship and praise (hymn 1 is "Praise to the Lord, the Almighty"), then moves through the Father, the Son and the Holy Spirit, the Scriptures, the gospel, the church, the Christian life and the Second Coming.

That order suits a service. A song leader can say "turn to 109" and everyone knows where to go, with no need for a title.

## Why the number matters on screen

In the pews, the number is the shortcut. People find the page, find the hymn and start singing.

On the screen, the number should do the same job. Your media team gets "109" from the song leader or the bulletin, and the slide should be ready in seconds. Three things make that hard in practice:

1. **Typing out verses by hand.** It is slow, and one typo shows up in front of the whole church.
2. **Searching by title.** The leader gave you a number. You then have to work out the title.
3. **Missing the chorus or repeat.** Hymns that repeat a refrain are easy to get out of order.

A good setup lets you search by the number and shows the full hymn, verse by verse, in the right order.

## A few tips for Sabbath morning

- **Build the schedule early.** Ask the song leader for the hymn numbers before Sabbath and add them to your schedule on Friday.
- **Keep verses short on screen.** Two or four lines per slide is easier to read from the back of the sanctuary.
- **Follow the congregation.** Some churches skip a verse. Keep the slides in the schedule so you can jump to the next one.
- **Have a spare.** Keep two or three well-loved hymns ready in case the program changes.
