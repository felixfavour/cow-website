### When to Use This Cue Sheet

Use this cue sheet whenever your worship set moves between different slide formats or involves musical shifts that a volunteer cannot predict by ear alone. It is designed for two people: the worship leader on the platform and the slide operator in the booth.

Keep this sheet on the music stand and next to the media keyboard during your mid-week rehearsal and your Sunday morning soundcheck. It prevents the slide operator from guessing whether you are repeating the bridge or going straight to the final chorus. It also gives the tech volunteer exact words and musical cues to watch for when you move between songs, scripture readings, and prayers.

### The Transition Cue Sheet Template

Copy and paste this template into a document, print it on a single sheet of paper, or paste it directly into your shared planning notes.

| Item / Song Title | Key & BPM | Platform Trigger (What the operator hears or sees) | Screen Output (Slide type and text trigger) | Stage Display & Notes |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 1. Countdown / Pre-Service | N/A | Band takes positions on stage at 1:00 remaining | Countdown timer video on live output screen | Stage clock starts running at 0:00 |
| 2. Song 1: [Title] | [Key] / [BPM] | Drum count-in (4 clicks on hi-hat) | Lyrics: Verse 1 slide ready before first sung note | Stage display shows current slide and next slide lyrics |
| 3. Transition: Scripture | Pad in [Key] continues | Leader speaks: "Hear the word of the Lord from..." | Bible verse slide (NKJV). Fire slide as leader reads reference | Stage display shows running service clock |
| 4. Song 2: [Title] | [Key] / [BPM] | Acoustic guitar introduces riff directly from pad | Lyrics: Slide changes on the first syllable of each line | Highlight repeat cues on Bridge (2x standard, 1x soft) |
| 5. Transition: Spoken Prayer | Piano plays soft chords under speaking | Pastor walks up to the center microphone | Blank hold slide or ambient background (no text) | Pause or reset stage countdown if pastoral prayer runs open |
| 6. Song 3: [Title] | [Key] / [BPM] | Worship leader sings first line solo without band | Lyrics: Advance immediately to Verse 1 | Big build on Chorus 2; do not lag behind tempo |
| 7. Hand-off to Sermon | Instrumental fades out | Pastor steps to pulpit and opens Bible | Text slide: Sermon title, speaker name, and main scripture | Switch stage display to sermon countdown timer |

### Filled-In Example: 20-Minute Worship Set

Here is how this template looks when filled out for a complete Sunday opening set that moves directly from music into a scripture reading, a response song, and the sermon hand-off.

| Item / Song Title | Key & BPM | Platform Trigger (What the operator hears or sees) | Screen Output (Slide type and text trigger) | Stage Display & Notes |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 1. Welcome Countdown | Track / Click | Band stands at instruments; stage lights come up at 0:30 | 5-minute countdown video with background audio | Stage clock counts down to 10:00 AM start |
| 2. Song: "Praise" | Key of G / 127 BPM | 4-count drum stick click | Lyric slide: "Let everything that has breath..." | High tempo. Change slides one word before the line begins |
| 3. Song: "Gratitude" | Key of B / 78 BPM | Leader swaps acoustic for electric; organ pad sustains in B | Lyric slide: "All my words fall short..." | Bridge repeats 3 times. Watch leader's hand signal for drop down |
| 4. Scripture Reading | Soft keyboard pad continues | Leader says: "Let us read together from Psalm 100:1-3" | Bible slide: Psalm 100:1-3 (NKJV). Switch slide on verse 2 | Keep text on screen until leader says "Amen" |
| 5. Song: "Doxology" | Key of G / 96 BPM | A cappella intro: Leader sings "Praise God from whom..." | Hymn slide: Standard four-line layout | Whole room singing; keep slides steady and advance on line 3 |
| 6. Pastoral Prayer & Hand-off | Soft acoustic strum | Associate pastor walks to lectern | Slide background only (no lyrics); fire parent alert overlay if needed | Reset stage timer to 35:00 for the sermon |

In this example, the slide operator does not need to guess when "Gratitude" ends and the scripture begins. The audio trigger ("Let us read together from Psalm 100") tells the tech volunteer the exact moment to pull up the Bible slide.

### How to Adapt This Sheet for Your Team

#### 1. Prioritize Audio Cues Over Visual Cues
Slide operators look back and forth between their monitor screen, the congregation, and their software interface. They rarely stare at the worship leader's hands for an entire service. Use distinct audio triggers in your sheet. A spoken sentence, a specific keyboard pad, a drum count, or a sudden change in instrumentation works far better than "worship leader nods head."

#### 2. Specify the Slide Type for Every Move
Avoid writing simply "change screen." Name the specific slide type so the tech operator can prepare the right element inside their software schedule. In Cloud of Worship, an operator works with distinct slide types including lyrics, Bible verses from specific translations like the NIV or NKJV, countdown timers, media videos, and text slides. If an item switches from song lyrics to a Bible verse, identifying that slide type on the cue sheet helps the volunteer locate it instantly in the schedule drawer.

#### 3. Match the Stage Display to Platform Needs
The stage screen facing the band needs different information during transitions than the congregation sees. Use the rightmost column of your sheet to define what the stage monitor should do. If your worship team relies on a stage clock or countdown timer to keep the service on schedule, note when that timer should start, pause, or reset. For instance, you can instruct the operator to keep the stage clock running during the song set, but switch to a speaker countdown when the preacher begins.

#### 4. Keep Schedule Adjustments in Sync
Last-minute setlist changes happen during Sunday morning soundchecks. If you cut a chorus or swap a song order twenty minutes before doors open, update the sheet immediately. Because Cloud of Worship provides a shared workspace with real-time editing, the worship leader can adjust the song setlist and schedule items on a laptop or phone while the slide operator sees those changes appear on the booth computer without needing a manual file transfer.

#### 5. Limit Notes to One Page
If a cue sheet spans three pages, volunteers will miss transitions while flipping paper. Keep the document to a single page containing only the start triggers, the song transitions, and the hand-offs. The simpler the cue, the faster your tech team can execute it.
