Look at the back rows of your auditorium next Sunday. You might spot an older church member leaning forward, squinting at the screen, or giving up on singing altogether because the words blur together.

In a mid-size church with a team of four to eight media volunteers, consistency can be tough. One person on the rota prefers artistic video backgrounds, another uses tiny font sizes to fit eight lines on a slide, and another switches fonts every week. For members dealing with aging eyes, reading the screen becomes frustrating.

Scripture reminds us to honor older generations. Leviticus 19:32 says, "You shall rise up before the gray head and honor the face of an old man, and you shall fear your God: I am the Lord." Serving older members through your projection booth does not require an overhaul of your tech setup. It simply requires addressing a few common myths.

### Myth 1: Huge font is the only fix for readability

**The reality:** Font size matters, but line length and line spacing matter just as much. When volunteers make fonts massive—like 70pt or 80pt—text wraps awkwardly. Words break into two or three fragments per line. Older eyes must jump back and forth rapidly across the screen, which increases fatigue.

**What to do instead:** Keep song slides to a maximum of two to four lines per slide. Set your font size between 40pt and 52pt using a clear, clean sans-serif like Arial, Montserrat, or Helvetica. Give the text breathing room with 1.2 line spacing. If a chorus or verse has eight lines, split it across two slides.

### Myth 2: Motion backgrounds make services engaging for everyone

**The reality:** Swirling color clouds, rapid particle effects, and bright video loops can disorient older eyes. Conditions like cataracts and astigmatism cause light to scatter across the retina. When text sits on top of moving light and dark patches, the letters appear to swim.

**What to do instead:** Stick to still backgrounds or very slow, subtle motion. If your worship team plays an upbeat track, you do not need rapid flashing visuals behind the lyrics. ### Myth 3: Maximum contrast requires pure white text on pure black

**The reality:** While high contrast is necessary, 100% pure white (#FFFFFF) text set against pitch-black (#000000) on a bright projector creates an effect known as halation. The letters appear to bleed or glow around the edges, making words fuzzy to someone with older corneas or corrective lenses.

**What to do instead:** Soften the starkness. Use an off-white or soft cream font (#E6E6E6 or #F3F4F6) on a deep charcoal or midnight navy background (#111827). This maintains a sharp contrast ratio well above accessible readability standards without creating a blinding glare.

### Myth 4: Older members should simply sit closer to the front

**The reality:** Expecting seniors to move to the front three rows overlooks practical realities. Many older attendees prefer the back rows or aisle seats for easy access to exits and restrooms, or because of joint stiffness. Others sit where their extended families sit.

**What to do instead:** Institute the "back row test." When the media volunteer arrives for the Sunday morning soundcheck at 8:30 AM, have them walk to the farthest corner of the sanctuary while test slides are on screen. If the scripture or lyrics cannot be read easily from the last two rows, the slides need adjustment before the doors open.

### Myth 5: You must print custom paper song sheets every week

**The reality:** Printing special paper lyrics sheets every week creates a heavy workload for church staff and media coordinators. When songs change on Thursday night or Sunday morning, printed papers end up outdated, leaving elderly members even more confused.

### Myth 6: Every volunteer on the rota should pick their own slide style

**The reality:** A rotating media team of six people often means six different aesthetics across the month. One volunteer likes serif fonts; another uses all-caps; another picks low-contrast pastel backgrounds. For seniors, this weekly unpredictability forces them to relearn how to decipher the screen every single service.

**What to do instead:** Create a simple style guide taped directly to the media desk:
1. Maximum 4 lines of lyrics per slide.
2. Sans-serif font only (such as Arial or Calibri).
3. Off-white text on dark backgrounds.
4. No italicized or all-caps blocks of text.
