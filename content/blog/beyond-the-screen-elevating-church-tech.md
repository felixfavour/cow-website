As a church administrator, you manage the software renewals, hardware purchases, and forgotten volunteer passwords that keep Sunday services running. Over twelve months, small changes pile up: unused cloud subscriptions renew on church credit cards, spare HDMI adapters vanish from the sound booth, and shared admin passwords sit unprotected on paper slips. This one-afternoon tech audit helps you review your entire setup in four focused hours, catching budget waste and gear risks before your next board meeting or Sunday morning scramble.

## 1:00 PM: Subscriptions, licenses, and billing

- [ ] Export your church bank and credit card statements for the last 12 months, filtering by tech, software, and audio-visual vendors.
- [ ] Cancel recurring monthly charges for software tools, stock media memberships, or video tools that your volunteers no longer open.
- [ ] Confirm your church holds active copyright licenses (such as CCLI or OneLicense) for lyric projection and online streaming, and record renewal dates.
- [ ] Consolidate presentation tools. For example, if you pay separate fees for lyrics, stage clocks, and Bible software, consider using Cloud of Worship, which includes whole-Bible search (KJV, NKJV, NIV, NLT, NASB, and YBCV) and built-in stage display tools in one platform.
- [ ] Verify non-profit tax exemptions with major suppliers so your church does not pay sales tax on monthly cloud subscriptions.
- [ ] Log into your domain registrar, verify ownership records, and set domain renewals to auto-renew on the church card.

## 2:00 PM: Passwords, accounts, and volunteer access

- [ ] Update the master passwords for your main presentation computer, streaming accounts, church Wi-Fi router, and YouTube channel.
- [ ] Store all system logins inside an encrypted team password manager rather than sticky notes or shared text files.
- [ ] Remove system access for former volunteers or staff members who no longer serve on Sunday media rotations.
- [ ] Set up individual user accounts instead of single shared logins where possible. Cloud of Worship supports shared workspaces where multiple operators edit the same service schedule simultaneously from their own logins.
- [ ] Verify that at least two staff leaders have two-factor recovery keys saved for church email and social streaming channels.
- [ ] Print one physical emergency login envelope with current passwords, seal it, date it, and file it in the church office safe.

## 3:00 PM: Presentation machines, displays, and cabling

- [ ] Turn on the presentation laptop or desktop and install all pending operating system updates so auto-updates never trigger on Sunday.
- [ ] Test every physical cable run from the computer booth to projectors, TV monitors, and stage display screens.
- [ ] Label both ends of each HDMI, SDI, Ethernet, and audio cable with colored tape and bold text.
- [ ] Test the primary projection feed alongside the stage display output to confirm lyrics and countdown clocks display properly on platform screens.
- [ ] Verify that presentation slides load without an active internet connection. Cloud of Worship runs in the browser and desktop app, caching your schedules so slides work offline if your sanctuary Wi-Fi drops.
- [ ] Check display resolutions on all connected TVs and projectors to eliminate black letterbox bars or stretched text.
- [ ] Clean dust out of computer fan vents, projector air filters, and power strip sockets using a can of compressed air.

## 4:00 PM: Backups, spare gear, and budget notes

- [ ] Back up your presentation media, song database, sermon slides, and graphic templates to an external hard drive or cloud storage.
- [ ] Inspect your spare hardware bin: confirm you have two fresh 9V batteries, two spare HDMI cables, a USB drive, and needed video display dongles.
- [ ] Test the emergency backup controller. With tools like Cloud of Worship, an administrator or pastor can control the live output directly from a smartphone if the mouse or keyboard freezes.
- [ ] Record the serial numbers and purchase years of all laptops, projectors, and video switchers in your church inventory spreadsheet.
- [ ] Draft a short summary list of aging gear that will need line-item budget replacement over the next 12 to 24 months.
- [ ] Send a brief update email to your pastor and treasurer noting completed cancellations and total monthly savings.

To adapt this checklist, print it out, strike through any items your church does not own, and schedule one afternoon on your church calendar each quarter to run through it.
